# H2: FOOTPRINT-MM, E's programme on B's pixel-scale likelihood

*Synthesist H2, 2026-10-05. Emphasis: the model, the objective and the features. No repository file was edited. All numerical checks were run in the scratchpad (`hybrid/h2/`, a private venv with LightGBM 4.7.0). Claims marked **[V-repo]** were checked against the repository, **[V-num]** were checked numerically here, **[V-rev]** come from a voting reviewer and were not re-checked, and **unverified** means nobody has checked them.*

---

## 0. Thesis in five lines

1. **Model (from B, repaired).** One habitat function F at 30 m pixel scale. It is learned through a footprint likelihood in which each checklist's encounter intensity is the kernel mean of exp F over its stencil. This is B's grouped cloglog model.
2. **Objective (new).** B's per-row Hessian is negative on 99.7% of detection rows [V-num]. R3's Gauss–Newton clamp is positive but under-counts curvature by a factor of up to M (7 or 13), so its steps overshoot. H2 uses a **majorised Gauss–Newton diagonal**, H_m = s_m·ℓ''(η). It is provably ⪰ the true Hessian, and per checklist it sums exactly to the one-row cloglog Hessian. On five synthetic worlds it beats both alternatives and E's stencil-mean model (§4).
3. **Features (V11, new).** Every column has one fixed radius. Categoricals become class fractions. One point-feature function is used both at the stencil points (training) and at every pixel (prediction), so training and prediction run at the same scale by construction.
4. **Data scope (V1, V2).** Checklists from 2020–2025 only, with a per-feature *true-vintage* gate. HH is built from `regions.block_split` and scored out of fold within 24 km super-blocks. Each fold model therefore trains on about 73% of all checklists, against 24% under E's flat 2.5 km buffer [V-num].
5. **Programme (from E, made power-aware).** E's H250 v0, its independent unshrunk fall model, its light stack and its product. B's fall-decided discipline is kept as a **fall non-inferiority gate**, which has power, instead of a fall superiority gate, which does not. The binding benefit gates are all-season gates (intersection–union), and an inconclusive result never kills the programme (§5).

## 2. Estimand

For covert C (a 2–40 ha polygon) and feature year τ:

$$\Lambda^\star(C,\tau)=e^{\,c+g(e^\star)}\cdot\frac1{|C|}\sum_{s\in C}\exp\!\big(h(x(s,\tau))\big),\qquad h=a\,F_{\text{all}}+b\,F_{\text{fall}}.$$

- **The standard walk e\*.** One median-skill observer, traveling, 60 min, 2 km, 15 October, 08:00, reference year, no observer effect. It affects only the scale. Ranking depends on h alone.
- **x(s, τ)** is the point-feature vector of §3.6 at pixel s.
- **The sum** is the same operation the likelihood applies to a footprint, with the covert's indicator in place of the Gaussian kernel. Training and prediction therefore apply one function F to one feature definition at one scale (V11).
- **Conversion** to flushes per hour uses a scalar κ fitted on the owner's logs (E/B). It is never used for ranking.

### 3.2 Footprint and stencil

The kernel and stencil come from B:
- footprint scale σ_j = √(ρ_ς² + (κ·D_j)²) ≤ 1.2 km, truncated at 2.5σ;
- ρ_ς ∈ {100, 150, 300, 600} m, chosen per season; κ ∈ {0.25, 0.35, 0.5};
- both are chosen by inner-CV deviance on training super-blocks only.

**Stencil.** One centre point, six points at 1σ and six at 2σ (offset by 30°), with a random rotation per checklist seeded from the checklist ID. Stationary checklists use the centre and the 1σ ring only (M = 7). Weights are the Gaussian annulus masses [0, 0.5σ], [0.5σ, 1.5σ], [1.5σ, 2.5σ], renormalised: centre 0.123, each inner point 0.097, each outer point 0.049. Log weights enter `init_score`.

### 3.3 Likelihood

For checklist j, with stencil rows m = 1…M_j:

$$r_{jm}=c+g(e_j)+\log w_{jm}+F(x_{jm}),\quad \lambda_{jm}=e^{r_{jm}},\quad \Lambda_j=\sum_m\lambda_{jm},\quad \eta_j=\log\Lambda_j,\quad s_{jm}=\lambda_{jm}/\Lambda_j,$$

$$P(y_j=1)=1-e^{-\Lambda_j},\qquad \ell_j=\begin{cases}\Lambda_j & y_j=0\\ -\log(1-e^{-\Lambda_j}) & y_j=1\end{cases}$$

Here s_jm is row m's softmax share of the checklist's intensity.

### 3.4 The objective: derivation and the V4 fix

All derivatives are of ℓ_j with respect to the row scores F_m. Write ℓ_η and ℓ_ηη for the derivatives with respect to η.

**3.4.1 Derivatives in η** (verified against finite differences, relative error ≤ 2.6 × 10⁻⁷ [V-num `v1_derivs.out`]):

| | ℓ_η | ℓ_ηη |
|---|---|---|
| y = 0 | Λ | Λ |
| y = 1 | −Λ/(e^Λ − 1) | Λ(Λe^Λ − e^Λ + 1)/(e^Λ − 1)² ≈ Λ/2 for small Λ |

ℓ is convex in η: ℓ_ηη ≥ 0 in both cases.

**3.4.2 The chain rule.** Since ∂η/∂F_m = s_m and ∂²η/∂F_m∂F_k = δ_mk s_m − s_m s_k:

$$G_m=s_m\,\ell_\eta,\qquad \mathbf H=\ell_{\eta\eta}\,\mathbf s\mathbf s^\top+\ell_\eta\,(\operatorname{diag}\mathbf s-\mathbf s\mathbf s^\top).$$

**3.4.3 Why B's Hessian is negative (V4).** The diagonal is H_mm = ℓ_ηη s_m² + ℓ_η s_m(1 − s_m).
- For a detection, ℓ_η ≈ −1, so the second term is ≈ −s_m(1 − s_m). The first term is only ≈ (Λ/2)s_m².
- This reproduces R3's and V4's expression, λ_m/(e^Λ−1)·[λ_m e^Λ/(e^Λ−1) − 1].
- On random checklists it is **negative on 1,969 of 1,975 detection rows (99.7%)** [V-num].
- The cause is structural, not numerical. −log(1 − e^{−Λ}) is convex and *decreasing* in Λ, and Λ is convex in F. A convex decreasing function of a convex function need not be convex. The detection NLL is genuinely non-convex in F, so the exact Hessian cannot be used as Newton curvature.

**3.4.4 Gauss–Newton (R3's clamp) is PSD but overshoots.** Dropping the ℓ_η·∇²η term gives H^GN = ℓ_ηη ssᵀ, whose diagonal is ℓ_ηη s_m².
- LightGBM sums diagonal Hessians over the rows in a leaf. When a checklist's whole footprint falls in one leaf, which is the common case in homogeneous cover, the leaf receives ℓ_ηη Σ s_m².
- The true one-leaf Gauss–Newton curvature is ℓ_ηη(Σ s_m)² = ℓ_ηη.
- With near-equal shares, Σ s_m² ≈ 1/M, so **the step is 7× too long (M = 7) or 13× too long (M = 13)** [V-num].

**3.4.5 The H2 Hessian: a majorised Gauss–Newton diagonal.**

$$\boxed{\,H_m = s_m\,\ell_{\eta\eta}\,}\qquad (G_m=s_m\,\ell_\eta \text{ unchanged})$$

*Claim 1: it is PSD and majorises both the Gauss–Newton and the exact Hessian.*
- For s on the simplex, diag s − ssᵀ ⪰ 0, because vᵀ(diag s − ssᵀ)v = Σ s_m v_m² − (Σ s_m v_m)² is a variance.
- Hence D := ℓ_ηη diag s ⪰ ℓ_ηη ssᵀ = H^GN.
- For y = 1, ℓ_η < 0, so H = H^GN + ℓ_η(diag s − ssᵀ) ⪯ H^GN ⪯ D.
- For y = 0, ℓ_η = ℓ_ηη = Λ, so H = Λ diag s = diag λ = D. **The exact Hessian is diagonal and H2 uses it exactly.**
- Verified: the minimum eigenvalue of (D − H_exact), relative to scale, is ≥ −1.2 × 10⁻¹³ over 400 random checklists; the minimum eigenvalue of (D − H^GN) is ≥ −5 × 10⁻¹⁶ [V-num].

*Claim 2: it is exact on a whole footprint.* Σ_m G_m = ℓ_η and Σ_m H_m = ℓ_ηη, verified to 4 × 10⁻¹⁶ [V-num `v5_reduce.out`]. Consequences:
- A leaf that holds a whole footprint takes exactly the one-row cloglog Newton step.
- **E's one-row model is the M = 1 case of the same code.**
- With identical features on every stencil row, the grouped and one-row boosters build identical trees. In the test, trees 0–6 matched exactly. The first difference came at a near-tie split caused by float32 gradient sums, and the final corr(F) was 0.989 [V-num]. The committed exact test is therefore at the objective level (the per-checklist sums), not the tree level.

*Claim 3: on a split footprint it is conservative.* When only part of a footprint (share S = Σ_{m∈leaf} s_m) is in a leaf, D gives curvature ℓ_ηη S, against ℓ_ηη S² for Gauss–Newton. The step is shortened by the factor S. It never overshoots, which is the standard majorise–minimise guarantee for the local quadratic model.

**3.4.6 Implementation rules** (each is a unit test in the acceptance CR):
1. **Intercept first.** Custom objectives do not boost from the average. If F starts at 0 while the effort scale is wrong, every row wants the same shift. G/H is then constant across rows, every split has non-positive gain, and LightGBM stops after one single-leaf tree. This was observed: the first run produced constant predictions. Fit c by one-dimensional Newton on Σ_j ℓ_η(Λ_j e^c) = 0 and add it to `init_score`.
2. **LightGBM 4.7 passes `init_score` inside `preds` to a callable objective.** Verified (`v0_initscore.py`). The objective computes Λ from `preds` directly.
3. **Rows contiguous per checklist; no row bagging and no GOSS.** Row bagging would drop rows from a group whose gradients assume the full group.
4. **Scale `min_data_in_leaf` by the mean M**, and **set `max_delta_step`** (pre-registered at 2.0). A leaf of detections alone has an unbounded optimum, as under logistic separation.
5. **Tests:**
   - gradient against finite differences;
   - **Hessian against finite differences** (B tested only gradients; R3);
   - the eigenvalue ordering D ⪰ H_exact on random checklists;
   - per-checklist sums equal to the one-row G and H (exact predicate);
   - a broken-pipeline test (PA-0021(a)): the exact-diagonal Hessian must make the T-obj fit fail its deviance threshold. In §4 it diverges after one tree.

### 3.5 The effort term g, and how it enters

**Covariates** (the event-only rule shared by E and B):
- log duration and log(1 + distance), both monotone non-decreasing (shape-constrained P-splines);
- protocol (including `hunt` from 2026), and party size;
- start-time spline and cyclic day-of-year spline;
- year as a factor (2020–2025), which absorbs population cycles;
- **observer skill computed out of fold by super-block** (Kelling et al. 2015), so no skill estimate uses checklists from the fold being scored;
- a ridge-penalised observer effect for observers with ≥ 20 training checklists.

g contains **no location-derived variable** (road, hotspot, trail or density) and **no species count**.

**How g enters.** It is an additive term on every stencil row's log intensity: r_jm = c + g_j + log w_jm + F(x_jm). Because it is shared by the checklist's rows, it factors out of the softmax, so s_jm does not depend on g. **g scales the footprint's intensity but cannot reweight where inside the footprint the habitat signal is credited.** That is why effort cannot be laundered into F through the footprint geometry.

**Fitting, by block-coordinate descent** (3 rounds, pre-registered):
1. With F fixed, fit g as a penalised cloglog GLM with offset o_j = c + log Σ_m w_jm e^{F(x_jm)}. Each checklist is a single row here, because the offset already aggregates the footprint. The fit is convex.
2. With g fixed, boost F **from scratch** with `init_score` = c + g_j + log w_jm. Re-fit the intercept c first (§3.4.6).
3. **Identifiability.** F and c trade a constant. After each boost, set F ← F − m̄, where m̄ is the mean of F over a fixed, committed reference sample of 100k forest pixels from 2020–2025, and set c ← c + m̄. g's intercept is fixed at its reference levels.

**At prediction,** g is fixed at the standard walk e\* (§2). It changes the displayed Λ\*, never the ranking.

**Residual risk.** g and F are fitted on the same rows, so a covariate correlated with both (long walks in big woods) is split between them by the alternation. The label-permutation band (§5) is the test that F is not carrying effort.

### 3.6 Features (V11)

**One function, `point_features(stack, s, year)`, defines every column.**
- At training it runs on the stencil points through the existing patch reader.
- At prediction the same function is vectorised as tile-wise box filters (cumulative sums on the GPU) with a 1 km halo.
- An exact-predicate **parity gate** requires the two paths to agree to 1 × 10⁻⁵ at 10,000 random pixels per region. If they disagree, the "same scale" claim is void.

**Fixed windows (square, odd, on the 30 m grid):** 3, 9, 21 and 65 px, which is 90 m, 270 m, 630 m and 1.95 km. The 21 and 65 px windows match `diagnose_gbm_baseline.py`'s `CAT_WINDOW` and its 64-px window, made odd so they have a centre [V-repo]. Every column has exactly one window. No column's scale varies with the checklist, which was C's and D's error.

**Columns, about 165 in total:**

| Block | Columns | Windows | Count |
|---|---|---|---|
| Continuous structure: `cc`, `ch`, `mch_mean`, `mch_f01`, `mch_f15`, `mch_f512`, TreeMap ×4 (if the vintage gate passes), slope | mean at all four windows; SD at 9, 21 and 65 | 4 + 3 | ~77 |
| **Class fractions** (V11): `evt` grouped to about 12 ecological groups (aspen–birch, northern hardwood, spruce–fir, white pine–hemlock, regenerating/shrub, alder and shrub wetland, herbaceous/old field, developed, agriculture, water, other forest, other); `nlcd` grouped to 8; `sclass` to 6 | fraction of the window in each class | 9, 21, 65 | ~78 |
| `evh` and `evc` | **numeric midpoints** of each code's range, via the LANDFIRE attribute tables (`download_attribute_tables.py`; **mapping to be verified in the features CR**), then means | 9, 21, 65 | 6 |
| Clock (from `tsd`) | share of the window aged 0–4, 5–10, 11–20 and 21+ years or undisturbed since 1999; Shannon diversity of age classes | shares at 9, 21, 65; diversity at 21, 65 | 14 |
| `mch` staleness | share of the window disturbed after 2020 | 9, 21 | 2 |
| Excluded | `road_dist`, `tcc`, coordinates, any checklist density | — | — |

**Why the class fractions are correct and the averaged codes were not.**
- The kernel mean of a fraction is the kernel-weighted fraction over the union of windows, a well-defined quantity.
- The mean of EVT codes is not a well-defined quantity (R3).
- In the grouped model no feature is averaged at all: each stencil row carries its own point features. The fractions matter for E's M = 1 arm and for interpretation.

**Storage.** Rows = checklists × about 12 (a mix of M = 7 and M = 13) × 165 columns.
- At an **unverified** 0.5–0.9 M checklists for 2020–25, that is 6–11 M rows.
- As a LightGBM binned Dataset (uint8 bins) this is 1–1.8 GB. The float32 staging table is 4–7 GB, written once and read in chunks.
- No per-pixel training cube exists.
- The prediction rasters exist only for feature year 2025, tile by tile.

### 3.7 Prediction at training scale

1. For feature year 2025, compute x(s) at every 30 m forest, shrub or wetland pixel through the parity-tested path.
2. Compute F_all,k(s) and F_fall,k(s) for each fold k, then h_k = a·F_all,k + b·F_fall,k.
3. The covert score is log mean_{s∈C} exp(h(s)). It is reported with the 10–90% spread over the 5 folds × 2 seeds.

**Why this is on-support.** The only inputs F ever sees, at training or prediction, are point-feature vectors x(s) built by the same function. Stencil points are spread over 1–2.5σ around start points, so they sample the near-trail forest, not only the trail. What remains off-support is geographic (V12), not a change of support.

**What F can resolve.** Fine-scale variation in F is identified only through contrasts between footprints, mostly from short stationary checklists (σ ≈ ρ_fall, 100–300 m). The effective resolution is about ρ_fall, not 30 m. Covert scores aggregate over 2–40 ha, which is within that resolution. §4 tests ranking at a 150 m covert scale.

### 3.8 The fall model, and how it enters

| Model | Rows | Footprint | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| **F_all** | All 2020–25 checklists | ρ_spring and ρ_fall by season | Most data, lowest variance. Spring audibility can tilt it. |
| **F_fall** | Checklists from 15 Sep to 30 Nov only, 2020–25 | ρ_fall | **Independent:** no offset from F_all, no shared trees, ordinary regularisation by fall inner-CV deviance (E). Same g (fitted on all rows), same features, same cross-fit folds. |

The fall window starts on 15 Sep because September is pre-shuffle and December is winter roosting (R5). 15 Sep–30 Nov keeps most of the fall volume while excluding both. It is pre-registered and never tuned on HH.

**Stack (the default ranking function).**
- h = a·F_all + b·F_fall, with a, b ≥ 0.
- (a, b) are fitted by maximising the grouped cloglog likelihood on **out-of-fold** fall checklists *within the training super-blocks* (nested; never HH). The fit has two parameters and an intercept, by L-BFGS from (1, 0) and (0, 1), keeping the better.
- If F_fall carries no information beyond F_all, then b → 0 and h ≈ F_all. If spring tilts F_all, then b grows.
- This replaces E's two-branch rule, whose 0.8 switch thin fall data cannot place reliably.

**Agreement statistic (E's test, still able to fail).**
- ρ_agree = Spearman(F_all, F_fall) over HH coverts.
- The threshold τ comes from T2 injection: ≥ 50 draws with no tilt and ≥ 50 with a planted spring audibility tilt, plus a **use-shift truth** (B's critique of E) where fall density shifts toward young cover.
- τ is set where the two distributions separate (PA-0021(c)). If they overlap, the statistic is demoted to an observation.
- When ρ_agree < τ, coverts whose F_all and F_fall quintiles differ get the "audible, not flushable?" badge.

**Demonstration** [V-num `v6_tilt.out`; 6 draws per arm, so illustrative only; the real calibration needs ≥ 50]: see §4.3.

### 3.9 E's one-row arm

Identical code with M = 1. Its features are the kernel-weighted means of the same point features, plus σ_j as a column so the model knows its own scale. Its roles:
1. A T1 arm. It is cheap and shows how much the grouped likelihood adds.
2. The fallback if any objective unit test fails.
3. A sanity bound: if the grouped model loses to it on inner-CV deviance, that is a defect to investigate before T1.

It is never predicted at single pixels: §4 shows that loses at the hunter's scale. When it is used for a map, it is predicted at the smallest trained σ (stationary, ρ_fall) with the kernel-mean features at that σ.

### 3.10 Cross-fit, HH and buffers (V2, V6)

1. **HH blocks.** `regions.block_split(all 3 km block ids covering ME/NH/VT, block_assignments.csv)`, where "val" marks HH [V-repo `regions.py:270-286`].
   - Exact-predicate test: the HH set equals this call's output.
   - Broken-pipeline test: building HH from the csv alone must give a strictly smaller set and must fail the predicate (V6).
2. **Super-blocks.** 24 km = 8 × 8 of the 3 km blocks, on the `regions.py` grid origin, so every HH block nests in one super-block. Five folds are assigned by an md5 hash of `SPLIT_SEED` and the super-block id.
3. **For fold k:**
   - *test* = HH checklists in fold-k super-blocks;
   - *train* = all checklists in the other folds, HH ones included, minus those whose start lies within r_j + 0.5 km of a fold-k super-block, where r_j = 2.5σ_j (B's footprint-aware rule).
   - The CNN is still scored only on HH, where it is out of sample.
4. **Retention** [V-num `v3_buffer.out`; uniform starts, so this is a geometry estimate only]:

| Scheme | Share of all checklists in each training set |
|---|---|
| E/C/D flat 2.5 km buffer around every HH block | **0.24** (reproduces R2/R4) |
| Flat 2.9 km | 0.18 |
| **H2 cross-fit, 2.5 km edge buffer** | **0.73** (0.91 of the other folds) |
| H2 cross-fit, 2.9 km edge buffer | 0.72 |

5. **Every number that decides anything is computed on HH out of fold.** Hyperparameters, (ρ, κ), the stack weights and the rounds come from inner CV on the training super-blocks only.
6. **Product.** The 5 fold models are averaged for the map, together with a 6th model fitted on everything after the gates. The gates are always decided on the out-of-fold models.

### 4.2 Boosting on a synthetic 30 m world (`v2_boost.py`; 5 seeds)

**Set-up.**
- **World:** 18 × 18 km. Truth F\* is at pixel scale, a function of young cover at 90 and 240 m, unimodal canopy and wetland.
- **Checklists:** 24,000, clustered at 60 "hotspots", with σ ∈ {150, 300, 500, 700} m.
- **Labels** are drawn from the *continuous* Gaussian kernel mean of exp F\*, so stencil quadrature error counts against the model.
- **Training and test:** train on the west half, test on the east half with a 900 m gap.
- **Fitting:** LightGBM, 400 rounds, learning rate 0.05, 15 leaves, g treated as known.
- **Scoring:** at a 150 m (5 × 5 px) covert scale.

| Arm | Held-out NLL (oracle 0.5040) | Spearman with true covert intensity | Spearman within top-decile young-cover coverts |
|---|---|---|---|
| B as written (exact diagonal) | **97.2** (diverges; stops after 1 tree in every seed) | 0.474 | 0.077 |
| R3's Gauss–Newton clamp (s²) | 0.5309 | 0.776 | 0.542 |
| **H2 majorised GN (s)** | **0.5129** | **0.893** (min 0.887) | **0.731** (min 0.570) |
| E as written (one-row, predicted at pixels) | 0.5243 | 0.859 | 0.646 |
| E "same scale" (one-row, predicted at the 300 m kernel) | 0.5243 | 0.674 | 0.627 |

**Reading.**
- The H2 Hessian beats every alternative in held-out deviance in all five seeds. Its gap to the oracle is 0.009, against 0.020 for E and 0.027 for GN.
- It has the best covert-scale ranking in 4 of 5 seeds (E-pix wins seed 0, 0.914 vs 0.897), and the best mean.
- Within the top decile of young-cover coverts, where hunters choose, it wins in 4 of 5 seeds.
- E's literal fix for V11 (predict at the training kernel) keeps the scale honest but blurs the map: 0.674. **Only the pixel-scale likelihood fixes V11 without that cost.**
- GN's map is noisier: max |F| ≈ 10–11, against 5–6.5 for MM. This is the overshoot of §3.4.4.

**Limits of this check.**
- The truth is inside the feature set, and g is known.
- Real gaps will be smaller and noisier.
- What the check establishes is the *direction* of the effect and the *correctness of the optimiser*, not the size of the real gain.

### 4.3 Fall model under a planted spring tilt (`v6_tilt.py`)

**Set-up.** The §4.2 world, with 30% of checklists in fall. Spring intensity is exp(F\* + TILT·(−canopy₆₀₀ₘ)), an audibility tilt toward open canopy; fall intensity is exp F\*. F_all and F_fall are both fitted with the H2 objective. Training sees 660–870 fall detections, fewer than the real estimate.

The stack column uses equal weights a = b = 1, a crude stand-in for the fitted stack.

| Truth | ρ(F_all, F_fall), range over 6 draws | ρ(F_all, fall truth) | ρ(F_fall, fall truth) | ρ(stack a=b=1, fall truth) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| No tilt | 0.855–0.925 | 0.861–0.906 | 0.813–0.868 | 0.861–0.918 |
| Tilt 1.0 | **0.401–0.691** | 0.500–0.649 | 0.813–0.868 | 0.766–0.871 |

**Reading.**
- **The agreement statistic separates the two truths.** The two ranges do not overlap, so E's test can fail.
- Under no tilt, pooling is at least as good as F_all in 4 of 6 draws.
- Under tilt, F_all loses about 0.3 Spearman. F_fall and the pool recover most of it.
- So the fitted stack (b → large when tilted) is the right default.

**Limits.**
- Six draws per arm is an illustration, not a calibration. PA-0021(c) needs ≥ 50 per arm on real EBD geometry, plus the use-shift truth, in T2.
- A weaker real tilt would narrow the gap between the ranges.

### 4.4 Power (`v4_mde.py`)

**Method.** Block-clustered negative-binomial counts (gamma k = 2 over blocks of 4 expected detections), with the two maps' top-5% sets overlapping by 40%. Block bootstrap. One-sided α = 0.05 at 80% power.

| Detections in the comparator's top 5% | SE(log TkL ratio) | MDE, superiority (ratio) | MDE, harm (ratio) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 30 | 0.205 | 1.67× | 0.60× |
| **100 (≈ fall HH, V7)** | **0.109** | **1.31×** | **0.76×** |
| 300 | 0.063 | 1.17× | 0.85× |
| 1,000 (≈ all-season HH 2020–25, **estimate**) | 0.035 | 1.09× | 0.92× |

| Informative same-day pairs | MDE for ΔCC (paired, ρ = 0.5) |
|---|---|
| 100 (≈ fall HH) | 0.124 |
| 300 | 0.072 |
| 1,000 | 0.039 |
| 3,000 (all-season HH, **estimate**) | 0.023 |

**Expected effects** (E's and B's priors): ΔCC 0.03–0.05; fall TkL ratio about 1.25×.
- **Fall superiority is not powered:** the MDE of 1.31× is above the expected 1.25×, and ΔCC needs about 1,000 fall pairs.
- **Fall harm is powered:** a drumming map that is ≥ 24% worse than the CNN in fall is caught 80% of the time with 100 detections.
- **All-season CC and TkL are powered** if day-1 counts confirm about 3,000 pairs and about 1,000 top-5% detections.

## 5. Gates: power against rigour, resolved

**Principle.** Bind on statistics that have power. Use fall where it has power: detecting *harm*. Report fall superiority as an estimate, never as a kill. A gate has three outcomes, and an inconclusive result never kills.

| Gate | Statistic, arena | Binding? | PASS | FAIL | INCONCLUSIVE |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **G1** benefit | Same-day case-crossover ΔCC (model − CNN), all-season HH, out of fold | **Binding** | One-sided 95% lower bound > 0 **and** model CC > the 95th percentile of the 100-refit permutation band | 95% upper bound < δ_CC (smallest effect of interest, 0.01, pre-registered) | otherwise |
| **G2** benefit | Effort-stratified ΔAUC, all-season HH | **Binding** | lower bound > 0 | upper bound < 0.005 | otherwise |
| **F1** fall harm | log TkL₅ ratio (model/CNN) on HH, 15 Sep–30 Nov | **Binding, kill-only** | lower bound > 0 ("fall-confirmed") | **upper bound < 0** (worse than the CNN in fall) | otherwise ("fall-unconfirmed") |
| F2 | Fall ΔCC, fall ΔAUC, Oct–Nov-only TkL | Advisory | — | — | Reported with CIs |
| P1 | First-visit CC: share of the G1 gain that survives | Advisory → badge | ≥ 50% | < 50% → "PS-fragile" badge | — |
| P2 | Permutation: any added feature block must raise real CC more than permuted CC | **Binding per block** | as stated | block dropped | block dropped |
| X | Cross-play (B), HH-X (A), off-trail TkL (E), control species (C) | Advisory | — | — | — |

**Combination rule (intersection–union, so no Holm).**

| G1 ∧ G2 | F1 | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| PASS | PASS | Ship the fitted map as primary, labelled "fall-confirmed". |
| PASS | INCONCLUSIVE | Ship as primary, labelled "fall-unconfirmed". H250 stays as a toggle layer; the random slot continues. F1 is re-tested at season end with the 2026 fall data and hunt checklists. |
| PASS | FAIL | Do not ship F_all. Try h = F_fall alone; it ships only if it passes F1 itself. Otherwise H250 stays. File a BUG ("drumming map"). |
| either FAIL | — | **Kill the checklist programme for this season.** H250, access and the ledger remain. |
| either INCONCLUSIVE, neither FAIL | — | Do not ship as primary. Keep H250, keep collecting, re-run at season end. **No kill.** |

**Error budget** (log scale, from §4.4, with 100 fall detections and a true fall ratio of 1.25):
- P(F1 FAIL | true benefit) = Φ((−0.223 − 1.645·0.109)/0.109) ≈ **0.0001**: a false kill is negligible.
- P(F1 PASS) ≈ **0.66**; P(INCONCLUSIVE) ≈ 0.34.
- P(F1 FAIL | the map is a 0.76× drumming map) ≈ 0.80.
- The G1 ∧ G2 conjunction has size ≤ 0.05 by intersection–union.

**What H2 accepts.**
- About a one-in-three chance of shipping a map labelled "fall-unconfirmed" when the model in fact works.
- A wrongly shipped map that is slightly worse in fall, between 0.76× and 1.0×, gets past F1 about 20–95% of the time, depending on its true ratio. H2 accepts this because such a map is still better all-season (G1 ∧ G2), and the random slot and the season-end re-test will expose it.

**All thresholds** (δ_CC, the permutation quantile, τ_agree) are calibrated in T2 on real EBD geometry with ≥ 50 fair and ≥ 50 broken draws (PA-0021(c)). If the fair and broken distributions overlap, the row becomes OBS (PA-0021(d)).

## 8. What H2 gives up

**Relative to pure E:**
- **Simplicity of the objective.** A grouped custom objective needs about 13× more rows and an alternation. *Acceptable*, because the same code covers E's model at M = 1, the tests are exact, and §4 shows a real gain at hunter scale.
- **E's season-contrast term** is dropped. *Acceptable*, because it was mis-identified (R2) and confounded (B, R5); F_fall carries its role.
- **2016–2019 data** are dropped (V1). *Acceptable*: the alternative is training on future landscapes.
- **About 1.5 weeks of schedule** over E's claimed plan. E's plan was already inconsistent, by R4 and R7.
- **E's AND kill rule** is replaced by intersection–union, which is stricter on benefit. *Acceptable*: it is powered.

**Relative to pure B:**
- **Fall superiority as a binding gate**, and **Holm**. *Acceptable*: at about 100 fall detections they are underpowered (MDE 1.31× against an expected 1.25×), so they would mostly produce false kills. Fall harm stays binding.
- **The shrunk Δ_fall head** is replaced by an independent F_fall. *Acceptable*, because Δ_fall cannot fail (E).
- **The cross-fitted disturbance panel as a gate** becomes a clock-only advisory (V3).
- **The 2016–2025 data span** and the DIST-ALERT fusion on day 2 are dropped (V1, V9).
- B's fail-closed posture is kept for *harm*, not for *unproven benefit*. **The cost is a one-in-three chance of shipping a fall-unconfirmed map.** It is labelled as such and backed by H250 and a randomised slot.
