# H3: FALL-STACK, a fall-anchored stack with power-aware gates and a hunter transfer ladder

*Synthesist H3, 2026-10-05. My emphasis is seasonality, detectability, and the transfer from birder detections to hunter flushes. Sections 3–6 cover that in depth; the rest of the proposal is complete but shorter. I edited no repository file. Each fact is marked **[V-repo]** (checked in the repository), **[V-web]** (checked by me on the web or through the GBIF API today), **[R]** (reported by a reviewer and not re-checked), or **unverified**.*

---

## 0. Thesis in five lines

1. **Ranking engine (from E).** E's light one-row LightGBM-cloglog model is the ranking engine. Its habitat rows use only checklists from 2020 on (V1).
2. **Detectability, decided by fall prediction.** Detectability is settled by out-of-fold prediction of fall-core checklists (October–November), not by asking whether two models agree. Three components are stacked: an all-season H with a season-offset term (E), E's unshrunk H_fall, and a new clock-only H_fall^clk that may legally use 2016–19 fall rows. Stack weights come from fall-core deviance. When the weights are inconclusive, a pre-registered minimax-regret weight, calibrated by injection, is used instead.
3. **B's fall-decided gates, run where they have power.** Fall gates that do not involve the CNN run on cross-fitted out-of-fold data over the whole area, which gives about 5× HH's fall sample. Only the CNN comparison stays on HH. Every gate has three outcomes: PASS, FAIL or INCONCLUSIVE. Only a FAIL stops anything.
4. **D's heard-vs-seen modes become a counted diagnostic, not a model head.** The owner's own flush logs are the real "seen" channel.
5. **Transfer from birders to hunters as a ladder.** In 2026 a scalar κ is fitted and a calibration slope b is tested, with reliability correction. A cover interaction ω is added only once there are 100 or more logged hunts. The scorecard serves as a prior and an independent scorer, never as a selector.

- The brood breaks up and juveniles disperse from mid-to-late September into October, over roughly 1–5 km. Females move about twice as far as males (4.8 vs 2.1 km in one study).

## 2. The fall power budget under the 2020+ floor

The assumptions are deliberately explicit, and the day-1 count replaces them:
- retention r = 0.55 / 0.65 / 0.75;
- EBD adds 20% for 2025;
- 16% of fall detections fall in a model's top 5% of forest;
- a block-bootstrap design effect of 2;
- correlation of 0.5 between two maps' top-5% errors.

The table gives the minimum detectable ratio of TkL₅ between two maps (one-sided α = 0.025, power 0.8), the power to detect E's expected ratio of 1.29× (2.0/1.55), and the probability of catching a map that is truly 0.8× as good.

| Window | Arena | Detections | Detections in top 5% | SE (log ratio) | MDE ratio | Power at 1.29× | P(catch 0.8×) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Oct–Nov | **HH** (20%) | 420–575 | 67–92 | 0.15–0.17 | **1.51–1.62×** | **0.32–0.41** | 0.25–0.33 |
| Oct–Nov | **OOF, all area** | 2,110–2,880 | 340–460 | 0.07–0.08 | **1.20–1.24×** | **0.91–0.97** | 0.83–0.92 |
| Sep–Dec | HH | 760–1,040 | 120–170 | 0.11–0.13 | 1.36–1.43× | 0.51–0.64 | 0.41–0.53 |
| Sep–Dec | OOF, all area | 3,800–5,200 | 610–830 | 0.05–0.06 | 1.15–1.17× | ≈ 1.0 | ≈ 1.0 |

**What this decides:**
1. **A fall gate on HH cannot be a binding PASS criterion.** Its power to see the expected effect is about 1 in 3. This is V7, made worse by V1. B's design, as written, would usually stop a working programme.
2. **The same fall gate on out-of-fold data over the whole area is well powered.** This is legitimate for every comparison *except against the CNN*. Only the CNN is trained on eBird positives outside HH (`sightings.py:20`). Effort-only, H250, the permutation null and the stack components are all cross-fitted by 25 km super-block (R2's V2 fix), so every checklist is scored out of fold.
3. **The fall comparison against the CNN on HH is kept, but only its FAIL arm binds.** A FAIL means the model is conclusively worse than the CNN in fall. The PASS arm is reported as advisory.
4. Widening to Sep–Dec buys power but mixes pre-shuffle and winter use. The **decision** window is therefore Oct–Nov. September 15–30 and December 1–15 enter **training** at weight 0.5 (§3.1) and never enter a decision.

The design effect of 2 is optimistic for out-of-fold data around hotspots. The harness measures it by super-block bootstrap, and if the measured MDE is worse than the decision-relevant effect, the gate becomes advisory automatically (§5).

## 3. The detectability treatment

### 3.1 What "fall" means here

| Window | Dates | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Fall core** | 1 Oct – 30 Nov | All fall decisions and metrics. It matches the hunting season's core; NH opens 1 Oct [V-web]. |
| Shoulders | 15–30 Sep, 1–15 Dec | H_fall training at weight 0.5. Never evaluated. |
| Off-fall | All other dates | H only, through the season-offset term |

Leaf-off arrives during the fall core, so detectability shifts within the season. The day-of-year spline in g absorbs the mean shift. A habitat-dependent leaf-off shift, for example in hardwood versus softwood, is **not** modelled, and I accept that residual. The hunter faces the same within-season shift, so the transfer ladder (§6) estimates it in aggregate.

### 3.2 Three stack components

All three share the event-only effort term g from E and B: log duration, log(1 + distance), protocol, party size, start-time spline, cyclic day-of-year spline, year, out-of-fold observer skill, and an observer random effect. g has no location input and no species count.

All three use the same stencil-mean feature construction, with the V11 fixes (§3.6). All three use the same 25 km super-block folds.

| Component | Rows | Features | Regularisation | Provenance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **H** | Every season, 2020+ | Full block: legacy LANDFIRE as class fractions, `mch_*`, clock shares at 90/250/600 m, elevation, SWE. TCC is excluded (§1). | Ordinary; chosen by all-season fold deviance | E |
| **H_fall** | Fall core + shoulders, 2020+ | A **pre-registered reduced set** of about 30 columns, chosen *a priori* and not from H's importances, so it stays independent of H: clock shares, guild and class fractions at 250 m, `mch_f15`/`mch` mean, `cc`, elevation, SWE | Ordinary, chosen by fall-core fold deviance. No offset from H and no shrinkage toward H. `min_data_in_leaf` ≥ 200 and ≤ 31 leaves are pre-registered as the *search bounds*. | E (unshrunk), with a new pre-registered feature set for thin data |
| **H_fall^clk** | Fall core + shoulders, **2016+** | Clock only: shares from `tsd` and Hansen; `nlcd` class fractions; elevation; SWE. Every one of these has a vintage within ±2 years of every 2016+ row (`tsd`/`nlcd` 2016–2025 [V-repo]; Hansen 2001–2024 [R]). The ingest CR **asserts this per row**. | Ordinary, by fall-core fold deviance | **New.** It adds about 40% more fall rows (Oct–Nov 2016–19 = 1,286 GBIF records against 3,198 for 2020–24 [V-web]) without breaching V1. |

**Why add H_fall^clk.** The V1 floor removes 2016–19 from every model that reads LANDFIRE. But the fall data are the binding constraint, and the clock is the feature family that carries the young-cover signal hunters want. A clock-only fall model can use 2016–19 legally. It is also the most transferable component off-trail, because the clock is defined everywhere and is not a birder-trail artefact.

### 3.3 The disputed season-offset term: what it can and cannot do

E's term is $\mathbb 1_{\text{spring}}\beta^\top o_j$, with fall as the reference. Reviewers raised three objections:
- **B and R5:** it assumes the same spring and fall habitat use.
- **R2:** it is identified cross-sectionally, not by repeat visits.
- **D:** it is linear in openness only.

**Analysis.** Write the spring hazard as λ_spring(Z) = λ_fall(Z) + δ(Z), where δ mixes audibility and any use shift. With fall as the reference level and β·o present only on spring rows:

1. **If δ lies in span(o)**, β absorbs all of it and H fits λ_fall. That holds *whether δ is detection or use*. **For the fall ranking, B's use-shift objection does no harm along the openness axis.** It does harm *interpretation*: β cannot be called "spring detectability", and a β-derived tilt map cannot be badged "audible, not flushable".
2. **If δ has components outside span(o)**, such as D's age-class interaction (drumming logs in 10–25-yr stands versus fall food cover in 5–15-yr stands), those components leak into H, weighted by the 60% spring share. That is the real damage, and it falls on the age axis that hunters care about.
3. **R2 is right about identification.** β is identified by the cross-sectional difference between the spring and fall openness slopes. The "≥ 500 cross-season repeat locations" power rule measured the wrong thing, so it is **dropped**. Power is the fitted SE of β plus the injection test.

**Resolution (a compromise between E and B):**
- **Keep the term as a nuisance absorber, renamed the "season offset". Claim nothing about detectability.** Its job is to keep spring- and summer-specific structure out of H, nothing more.
- **Widen it slightly** to address point 2: separate offsets for spring (Apr–Jun), summer (Jul–Aug, brood and gravel season) and winter (Jan–Mar), each linear in o = {`cc`, conifer fraction, young (0–10 yr) clock share, mid (10–25 yr) clock share}. That is 12 coefficients. Adding the two age shares lets the offset absorb the drumming-age versus fall-age shift D describes. **TCC is not in o**, because of the 2026 tolerance problem.
- **Do not trust it alone.** Because of point 2, H is never the sole fall ranker unless the stack says so (§3.4). This is how B's critique is honoured without dropping E's cheap absorber.

### 3.4 The decision rule: stack by fall-core prediction, calibrated by injection

This replaces both E's Spearman ≥ 0.8 switch and B's shrunk Δ_fall head.

**The stack.** On the linear-predictor scale:

$$h_{\text{fall}}(s)=a+w_0\,H(s;\text{fall})+w_1\,H_{\text{fall}}(s)+w_2\,H^{\text{clk}}_{\text{fall}}(s),\qquad w\in\Delta^2\ (\text{simplex}).$$

**Fitting the weights.**
- w is fitted by minimising **out-of-fold fall-core deviance**. Each component's prediction for a checklist comes from the fold that did not see it.
- The fit uses all non-HH area. HH stays untouched for the CNN comparison.
- An interval for w comes from 200 super-block bootstrap resamples.

**Why this can fail, and why it suits thin data.**
- The criterion is *predicting fall checklists*. That is the closest available proxy to the hunter's question, and it needs no assumption about whether a spring–fall difference is detection or use.
- If spring structure has leaked into H (point 2 above), H predicts fall checklists worse, and weight moves to H_fall and H_fall^clk.
- If there is no tilt, H wins on variance, because it has 5–8× more rows. That is the right answer for prediction: with no tilt, the spring rows are free information.
- A shrunk head cannot reach the "disagree" branch. A deviance-weighted stack can put weight anywhere on the simplex.

**Three outcomes for the weight decision** (pre-registered):

| Outcome | Condition | Ranking used |
|---|---|---|
| **Decided** | 95% bootstrap interval of the fall share (w₁ + w₂) has width ≤ 0.5 | Fitted w |
| **Inconclusive** | Interval width > 0.5 | **w_mm**, the minimax-regret weight from the injection grid (below), on a pre-registered grid of steps of 0.25 |
| **Gate absent** | Injection shows the fitted w cannot separate tilt from no-tilt truths (below) | w_mm, fixed. The fitted w is reported as an observation (OBS) only, per PA-0021(c). |

**How w_mm is set.** For each candidate weight on the grid and each injected truth, compute the recovery of the true fall top 5% on *unvisited* forest cells (B and A's scoring). The regret of a weight is the gap to the best weight for that truth. w_mm is the weight with the smallest maximum regret across truths.

**Prior expectation.** w_mm will lean toward the fall components, because the use-shift and age-interaction truths punish H heavily while the no-tilt truth punishes the fall components only mildly. **This is the formal version of E's "when underpowered, the stack is the default, not H".**

**E's agreement rule is kept, for badges only.**
- Over covert objects, compute the Spearman correlation between H(fall) and H_fall.
- Its threshold τ* is calibrated in T2: the 10th percentile of the no-tilt distribution over 100 draws.
- The rule is a **GATE for badges** only if its firing rate on the tilt truths at the planted size is ≥ 0.8. Otherwise it is an OBS, and no "audible, not flushable" badges ship.
- Coverts where H and H_fall disagree by more than one quintile receive the badge only when the rule is a gate.
- With thin fall data I expect it to be an OBS. That is an honest outcome, not a failure.

### 3.5 Injection test T2 (written before approval under CR-0011 A3; ≥ 100 draws per truth)

T2 runs on real 2020+ EBD checklist geometry and effort, with the real fall sample size (and 2016+ for H_fall^clk rows). Each truth defines λ_fall and λ_spring/λ_summer over real features:

| Truth | Construction | Expected behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| T-0, no tilt | λ_spring = λ_fall | w can sit anywhere. The agreement rule must stay silent in ≥ 90% of draws. |
| T-det, audibility | λ_spring = λ_fall · exp(γ·openness), with density highest in dense young cover (E's truth) | The offset absorbs it. All weights are fine. Tests that the offset works. |
| **T-use, use shift** | Fall density shifted toward young dense cover relative to spring (B's missing truth) | The offset partly absorbs it, through its age shares. Weight should move to the fall components if H is hurt. |
| **T-age, age interaction** | Spring peaks at 15–20 yr, fall peaks at 8–12 yr (D's scenario) | Weight must move to the fall components. **This is the critical truth.** |
| T-ps, preferential sampling | Visit odds rise near trails and decline in dense young cover (A/B) | Reported for the off-trail evaluation |

**Outputs, all committed:**
- the separation of fitted w between T-0 and T-age;
- w_mm;
- τ* and the agreement rule's firing rates;
- the deviance-stack interval width on real sample sizes, which predicts whether "Inconclusive" is likely;
- the MDE of each fall gate (§5).

Each threshold quotes both its fair and its broken distribution over 100 draws, as PA-0021(c) requires. Where they overlap, the row is demoted to OBS.

**Cost.** Each draw refits four models on about 10⁶ rows, roughly 5–10 CPU-minutes, so 5 truths × 100 draws is about 40–80 CPU-hours. This runs overnight in parallel on the EC2 host. If wall-clock time is short, draws use a 300k-checklist subsample that keeps every fall row, and the subsample is recorded.

### 3.6 Feature construction for the fall components (V11)

- Categorical codes (`evt`, `evh`, `evc`, `sclass`, `fdist`, `nlcd`) become **class fractions** over the stencil, collapsed to about 12 pre-registered guild classes. Codes are never averaged.
- Disc features use **fixed radius columns** (90/250/600 m), never "the radius nearest ρ + L/2".
- **Prediction support matches training support.** A covert is scored by the same 16-point stencil construction used for checklists. Its points are spread over the covert polygon (2–40 ha) and blurred by the fall footprint ρ_fall. ρ_fall is chosen from {100, 150, 300} m by fall-core deviance, which is C and B's season radius applied to the fall components only.

  Prediction is therefore never at a single pixel. The H_fall training rows are mostly short fall walks with σ ≈ 150–400 m, so a covert-scale stencil sits inside their support.

### 3.7 D's heard-vs-seen modes: are they worth it?

**Verdict: not as a model head. Yes as a day-1 count and a transfer-risk diagnostic.**

Reasons:
1. **The labels are missing not at random and seasonally confounded** (B, R5). The visual codes FL, DD, NY and CF are breeding-season brood codes. In fall, breeding codes are rarely entered (**unverified**; counted on day 1). An explicitly coded fall-visual subset will very likely fall below the 500-detection threshold that B and D themselves set.
2. **An unshrunk visual head fitted on a few hundred detections** would be the noisiest component, and its "visual" class is largely gravel birds on roads, which are not hunter flushes.
3. **The real "seen" channel is the owner's own flush log** (§6). It is the hunter's detection process exactly, so it supersedes eBird mode codes as the transfer instrument.

**The pre-registered admission rule is kept, so the question stays open honestly.** A visual-mode fall head becomes a fourth stack component only if both of these hold:
- there are ≥ 500 explicitly coded (breeding code or comment keyword) **fall-core** visual detections in 2020+;
- in T2, adding the head lowers the maximum regret by more than the bootstrap SE.

**What is always reported (OBS):** the fall-core mode mix. This is the share of coded fall detections that are aural (fall drumming), visual-on-road ("road", "gravel", "crossing") and visual-off-road. A fall eBird sample dominated by drumming or road gravel predicts a weak transfer slope b in §6, so the share is a leading indicator for the transfer ladder.

### 3.8 What happens to B's fall head and B's fall gates

- **B's Δ_fall head** (depth ≤ 3, strong L2, F frozen) is **dropped**. It cannot disagree with F (E's critique, confirmed by R2 and R3). H_fall and H_fall^clk replace it.
- **B's principle that "the fall number decides"** is **kept** for the two questions where it matters: which ranking function ships (§3.4) and the guard against a drumming map (gate G2 in §5). Each is run **where it has power**: out of fold over the whole area. Only the CNN comparison is confined to HH.
- **B's Holm adjustment on a conjunction** is **dropped** (R2). A conjunction of gates that must all pass is an intersection–union test and already holds level α. Holm is applied only where several *any-of* admissions are made, such as several optional feature blocks.

## 4. The rest of the design (whole, but shorter)

### 4.1 Data and arenas

- **Labels and effort.** EBD + SED for ME/NH/VT, using Johnston et al. (2021) filters. Habitat rows are 2020+ (V1). Clock-only fall rows are 2016+. Border strips are dropped for 2026. They need extra EBD requests, and the LCMS/LANDFIRE domain stops at the border (R6, PA-0017/0023).
- **HH.** CNN validation blocks taken from `regions.block_split(SPLIT_SEED=42, VAL_FRACTION=0.2)` over *all* blocks, not from `block_assignments.csv` (V6). A test asserts that its set equals `generate_negatives.py`'s assignment on occupied blocks.
- **Cross-fitting (V2, R2).** 25 km super-blocks are grouped into 5 contiguous folds. Fold k trains on the other folds, with a 2.5 km exclusion **only along fold boundaries**. That costs about 10% of training rows, an estimate the harness computes, compared with about 70% for the flat HH buffer.
  - Every checklist, HH included, is scored out of fold.
  - The CNN is scored on HH only.
  - The temporal arm trains on ≤ 2023 and tests on 2024–25 (C, R4).

### 4.2 Model

- **One-row stencil-mean LightGBM-cloglog** (E), with g passed as `init_score` and alternated 3–5 times.
- The row-wise cloglog Hessian is non-negative but tiny on detection rows (R3), so `min_sum_hessian_in_leaf` is set as a pre-registered floor.
- **There is no grouped log-sum-exp objective** (V4 cannot arise). A finite-difference test of **both gradient and Hessian** is committed anyway.
- The ensemble is 5 folds × 2 seeds. The fold spread gives the epistemic interval, which §6 reuses for the reliability correction.

### 4.3 Clock (v1 for 2026)

- `tsd`, regenerated by CR-0035, plus Hansen GFC `lossyear` fetched on its **source lattice** and warped locally through the CR-0034 functions (V5).
- LCMS (v2025-11 [R]), HF437 (Maine only, so it would bias the clock by state; V9) and OPERA DIST-ALERT (not in Earth Engine; V9) are **out of 2026 scope**.
- 2024–26 cuts are therefore invisible in 2026. The product's freshness badge says so.

### 4.4 Product

1. **Week 1: H250 v0.** The share of a 250 m radius cut 5–20 years ago, from `tsd` already on disk (R4). Cuts from 2006–2021 do not need fresh-cut data. It carries PAD-US access classes A1–A4/X, never shows A4 as open, and exports GPX/KML. It is frozen and hashed before any test, and rebuilt once from the CR-0035 `tsd`, with the hash change logged.
2. **Fitted layer.** Covert objects (D/E). Each card shows h_fall rank and κ-scaled flushes per hour with an 80% interval, peak window from the clock, access class, walk-in distance and top-3 reasons. Badges:
   - "fall-unverified" (G2 INCONCLUSIVE);
   - "audible, not flushable" (only if §3.4's rule is a gate);
   - "PS-fragile";
   - **support**, geographic: eBird track density within 500 m (R1, R5, V12);
   - "clock blind to 2024–26 cuts".
3. **Daily list.** Four coverts by Thompson sampling plus **one randomised slot, drawn from accessible coverts in h_fall quintiles 2–5**. This is changed from D's top-30% draw so the transfer slope has leverage (§6.3).

## 5. Gates: which bind, which advise, and what INCONCLUSIVE does

**Every gate reports one of three outcomes**, set by one-sided 97.5% bootstrap bounds (super-block bootstrap, ≥ 200 resamples) against a pre-registered decision-relevant effect m:
- **PASS:** lower bound > 0, or > the margin;
- **FAIL:** upper bound < 0, or the statistic lies inside the permutation band;
- **INCONCLUSIVE:** otherwise.

No gate passes on "> 1 SE" (V13).

**Day-1 MDE rule.** Before any real fit, T0 counts and T2 injection give each gate's MDE. A gate whose MDE exceeds its m is **auto-demoted to OBS**, and the demotion is recorded in the CR. This is the "compute MDE before binding" adoption from R1, R5 and V7.

| ID | Question | Arena / window | m (decision-relevant) | Status | FAIL → | INCONCLUSIVE → |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| G0 | Does the model beat effort-structure noise? Real all-season CC above the p95 of **100** within-stratum permutation refits (V8) | OOF all area | — (band) | **BINDING** | Stop the checklist programme; keep H250 | n/a (band is exact-ish) |
| G1 | Better within-day ranking than the CNN (all-season CC) | HH | 0.01 CC | **BINDING** if MDE ≤ 0.02; otherwise the fallback is effort-stratified AUC (E's rule), at m = 0.01 AUC | Stop the programme | Ship as "no gain over CNN shown"; random slot decides at season end |
| **G2** | **Drumming-map guard:** fall-core TkL₅ of the shipped stack vs H250 and vs effort-only | **OOF all area, Oct–Nov** | 1.10× | **BINDING** (expected MDE 1.20–1.24×, §2; if T0 shows MDE > 1.3×, the window widens to the shoulders and the gate is re-registered *before* fitting) | Do **not** ship the fitted map; H250 continues | Ship with the "fall-unverified" badge; Thompson prior mixes stack and H250 50/50 |
| G3 | Fall-core TkL₅ vs the CNN | HH, Oct–Nov | 1.15× | **FAIL arm binding, PASS arm advisory** (power about 0.35, §2) | No superiority claim over the CNN; ship only if G2 PASSes | Report |

**Error budget, stated plainly:**
- **False ship of a pure birder-geography map:** stopped by G0. The permutation band is an exact computed null.
- **False ship of a drumming map** (truly 0.8× H250 in fall): G2 catches it with probability about 0.83–0.92 (§2). If it slips through as INCONCLUSIVE, the ranking is half H250 and badged. **I accept a residual of roughly 10–15%**, bounded by the randomised slot and the post-season review.
- **False kill of a working programme:** G2 on HH alone would false-kill or stall about 60–70% of the time. Run out of fold over the whole area, a true 1.29× model reaches PASS with probability about 0.9 and a FAIL with negligible probability. A single binding test at one-sided 0.025 has a false-kill risk of ≤ 2.5%. Across the G0/G1/G2 conjunction it is ≤ 7.5%, plus the power shortfall, which routes to INCONCLUSIVE, not to a kill.
- **What I give up:** a fitted map can ship this season without proof that it beats the CNN *in fall*. That is the price of not stopping on an underpowered HH test.

## 6. Transfer from birder detections to hunter flushes: calibration by the owner's logs and scorecard

### 6.1 Why the transfer needs its own instrument

The fitted map ranks **fall birder encounter rate**. A hunter's detection process differs in four ways:
- **Channel.** Flush at close range in dense cover, against fall birders' mix of fall drumming, road gravel and trail flushes (§3.7).
- **Route.** Off-trail with or without a dog, against on-trail.
- **Pressure.** Public coverts get depleted (R1, R5).
- **Leaf-off timing.**

None of these is identifiable from eBird. R5's S1 point stands: no eBird statistic can falsify "eBird rank = hunter flush rank". Only hunter data can.

### 6.2 Logging protocol (frozen in the product-v0 CR, before the first hunt)

| Field | Why |
|---|---|
| GPX track at ≥ 0.2 Hz, start/end time, covert ID from the list, and whether the visit was the **random slot** | Effort; arm assignment |
| Waypoint per **distinct bird**, flagged flush-seen / flush-heard / drum-heard / seen-on-road | The hunter's own mode labels; this is D's decomposition done on the right population |
| Dog (Y/N), party size, weather (wind, precipitation), leaf state (on, mixed, off) | Hunter effort term g_h |
| Re-flush of the same bird marked as such | Distinct-bird counts match eBird's per-checklist detection |
| Covert polygon edits | Covert geometry for scoring |

**Contamination rule.** Hunts are logged **privately and not submitted to eBird**. If the owner already submits eBird checklists, the owner's observer ID is **excluded from all EBD training and evaluation rows** from 2026 on. The reason: once targeted-search checklists reach future EBD releases, they leak hunter-map choices into the labels, and the CNN's GBIF positives inherit them too (`sightings.py:20`). This replaces B's "hunts enter the same likelihood as eBird checklists". The owner's rows join through the transfer model below, never as eBird rows.

### 6.3 The transfer model and its ladder

For hunt visit k in covert c(k) with GPS hours t_k:

$$N_k\sim\text{NegBin}\Big(\mu_k=\kappa\,t_k\,\exp\big[b\,(h_{\text{fall}}(c_k)-\bar h)+g_h(\text{dog, party, weather, leaf, doy})+\omega\,u(c_k)+\xi\,\text{press}(c_k,t)\big],\ \phi\Big)$$

| Rung | Data needed (estimate) | Parameters fitted | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| L0 | none | κ prior from NH flush rates ([R] 1.3–2.3/h) and the physics check (A) | Display only, labelled "uncalibrated" |
| **L1, 2026 in season** | ≥ 20 visits | κ (scalar) | Display. **Ranking is unchanged.** |
| **L2, 2026 post-season** | ≈ 40–60 visits | **b, the calibration slope**, tested against ρ_rel (below) | **The transfer test** |
| L3, 2027+ | ≥ 100 visits | ω on **one** pre-registered structure covariate (young-share at 250 m, or `mch_f15`); ξ for pressure on A1 access × weeks since opener (A) | Enters the ranking as h_fall + ω·u for hunter use |
| L4, 2028+ | ≥ 200 visits | g_h refinements; a covert random effect | — |

**The reliability correction (new).** Even with perfect transfer, b is attenuated toward 0, because h_fall is estimated with error (errors-in-variables). Compute ρ_rel = 1 − mean(fold-ensemble variance of h_fall over coverts) / Var(h_fall over coverts). Then test b against ρ_rel, not against 1. Testing against 1 would misread estimation noise as transfer failure.

**Power** (my calculation: NegBin with μ ≈ 2–3 flushes per visit and φ = 1–2; σ_h = SD of h_fall across visited coverts):

| Visits | SE(b), σ_h = 0.3 (Thompson top only) | SE(b), σ_h = 0.5 | SE(b), σ_h = 0.7 (quintile-2–5 random slot) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 40 | 0.48–0.65 | 0.29–0.39 | **0.21–0.28** |
| 100 | 0.30–0.41 | 0.18–0.24 | 0.13–0.17 |
| 200 | 0.22–0.29 | 0.13–0.17 | 0.09–0.12 |

Two consequences:
- **Thompson sampling alone ranges-restricts the visits** (σ_h ≈ 0.3) and makes b almost unestimable in one season. That is why the random slot is drawn from quintiles 2–5 rather than from the top 30%. It roughly doubles σ_h at the cost of about 20% of visits in middling cover, while never sending the owner into the worst quintile.
- **With about 40 visits, 2026 can tell "transfers" (b ≈ ρ_rel) from "does not transfer" (b ≈ 0)**, which is 3–4 SE apart. It cannot tell full from partial transfer.

**Pre-registered decisions at L2 (2026 post-season):**

| Result | Action for 2027 |
|---|---|
| b's 95% CI excludes 0 and includes ρ_rel | Transfer holds. Keep the map. Fit L3 when the data allow. |
| b's CI excludes 0 but lies wholly below ρ_rel | Partial transfer. Widen the card intervals by 1/b. Prioritise the L3 cover term. |
| b's CI includes 0 | **Transfer not shown.** For 2027, the ranking becomes 50/50 h_fall and H250 (ecology), until 100 visits decide. The fall-core eBird mode mix (§3.7) is checked as the likely cause. |
| b < 0 with CI excluding 0 | Transfer reversed. File a BUG and investigate; go back to H250 as primary. |

**Separate gamma–Poisson covert effects** (D/E) are updated in season for *re-visit* decisions on individual coverts. They are kept out of the b estimate so the two do not double-count.

### 6.4 What the scorecard calibrates, and what it must not

The scorecard S0 is frozen before any map is seen: ≥ 30 past coverts drawn as polygons, each with a 1–5 rating and times hunted (D).

- **It does not choose models or weights.** With n ≈ 30, its SE is about 0.18, so only differences of 0.35 or more are visible. It is also biased toward visible young cover (A's critique), which favours H250 by construction.
- **It is an independent scorer.** Report the weighted Spearman for the CNN, H250, H, H_fall, H_fall^clk and the stack. Also report the **partial Spearman controlling for H250**. That isolates what the fitted map adds beyond what the owner can already see, and it neutralises the scorecard's young-cover bias.
- **It gives a prior for b before 2026 logs exist.** A cumulative-logit regression of rating on h_fall (weighted by times hunted) gives a slope direction b₀. That becomes a weak prior for b (N(b₀, 0.5²)), used only in the *display* intervals, never in the L2 test.
- **It gives an early transfer warning.** If the stack's S0 partial Spearman is negative and its CI excludes 0, that is advisory. The owner is told before switching from H250 to the fitted map on the switch date.
- **Upgrade path.** If past GPX tracks with flush waypoints exist, they become L2 data from day 1 (D). They are tagged "retrospective, non-random", and enter only κ and the display, not the b test.

## 10. What the hybrid gives up

**Compared with pure E:**
- **E's lenient AND-kill.** A model that wins all-season CC but is conclusively worse than H250 in fall now does not ship. Some programmes E would ship, this stops. Those are the drumming maps.
- **Habitat fitting on 2016–19 rows**, which V1 forbids anyway.
- **β as a detectability estimator and its tilt map.** It is now a nuisance offset, so "audible, not flushable" badges ship only if the injection-calibrated agreement rule becomes a gate. They probably will not in 2026.
- **The week-3 forecast** and **X2 in week 2**, both deferred.
- **About one week of schedule**, spent on T2's larger truth grid and on the 100 refits.

**Compared with pure B:**
- **A pixel-scale MIL habitat function** (R3's favourite). Support-matched stencil prediction replaces it.
- **"Every gate must pass on fall HH, Holm-adjusted."** Fall-vs-CNN on HH now binds only on FAIL. A map can ship without demonstrated fall superiority over the CNN.
- **The shrunk Δ_fall head.**
- **Hunts as eBird-likelihood rows.** They become a separate transfer model with a contamination rule.
- **Cross-play as a primary label test.** It is now OBS.

**Why this is acceptable.**
- The power table (§2) shows B's HH fall gate would stall a true 1.29× model about two times in three. That loses the season's learning while protecting against an error that G2, run out of fold over the whole area, already catches 83–92% of the time.
- E's looser rules would ship drumming maps that the fall-core guard now stops.
- Neither design had any instrument that measures the birder-to-hunter transfer itself. The ladder in §6 does, and the quintile random slot makes it estimable in one season.
