## Findings that apply across designs (checked myself; I did not see them in any round's critiques)

### 1. The HH training buffer destroys most of the training set (MAJOR for B–E; BLOCKING for A as written)

**How HH is built.** HH consists of the CNN's 3 km validation blocks. That is a random 20% of cells, scattered like a checkerboard. Every design then removes training checklists within a buffer of any HH block.

**Method.** I simulated the 3 km hashed grid at 20% validation, with checklist start points uniform over the area. I measured the share of *non-HH* checklists that each buffer removes:

| Buffer | Non-HH checklists removed | Design |
|---|---|---|
| 0.5 km | 14% | — |
| 1 km | 29% | — |
| 2 km | 59% | — |
| 2.5 km | **70%** | C, D, E |
| 2.9 km | **78%** | upper end of B's footprint-aware rule |
| 5.5 km | **98%** | **A** |

**Consequences.**
- C, D and E train on about 0.8 × 0.30 ≈ **24% of all checklists**.
- B, whose rule depends on each footprint, trains on roughly 25–45%.
- A's main fit sees about **1.6%** of checklists. Fall detections, which are the scarce resource every design depends on, shrink by the same factor.
- No design counts this loss, and no critic raised it. A's round-3 critique of B even asked for a *larger* buffer.

**Fix.** Cross-fit HH:
1. For fold k, HH_k = the CNN-validation 3 km blocks that lie inside the held-out 25 km super-blocks of fold k.
2. Train on the other super-blocks, buffering only at super-block edges.
3. Rotate over the 5 folds, so that every HH checklist is scored out-of-fold. The CNN is then still scored only inside its own validation blocks.

### 2. The panel tests (C's X2, adopted by B, D and E; A's T5) cannot see the ages they claim to test (MAJOR)

**The age bins have no data behind them.**
- **B, C, D and E** specify cuts from 2005 to 2019 and checklists from 2010 to 2025, with at least 8 checklists **before** the cut.
  - A cut in 2005–2010 has no checklists before it at all. C's leads −4…−1 push the first usable cut year to about 2014.
  - Northern New England eBird volume before about 2013 is thin.
  - So the identified post-cut ages run from about 0 to 11 years. C's 0–4 exclusion, which E and D also use, leaves only about **5–11 years**.
  - The 11–15, 16–25 and 26–40 bins are not identified within sites at all.
- **A** restricts its checklists to 2016–2025 (O1). T5 then sees ages from about 0 to 8, which is the rising limb only. A log-normal hump with a peak near 10 years cannot be estimated from that. A's key differentiator, "the age curve is estimated within sites and imposed", will therefore come back prior-dominated or underpowered.

**Features for the pre-cut years do not exist.**
- The model check needs $\hat F(x_{\text{pre}})$ for the years before each cut.
- For checklists before 2016, the legacy features do not exist. TCC starts in 2016 and TreeMap's first vintage is 2016.
- Mapping to the nearest vintage puts a post-cut 2016 TCC/TreeMap value into the "pre" period of a 2014 cut. That shrinks $\Delta\hat\eta$ toward 0 and biases the slope test.
- `mch_*` is static, so it contributes nothing to $\Delta\hat\eta$.
- **The fix:** compute the predicted change from the clock only, which is available from LCMS/Hansen back to 1985, or restrict to cuts from 2017 onward.

### 3. Under cloglog, the case-crossover does not cancel observer-day effects exactly (LOW)

- Conditioning on exactly one detection in a pair gives odds of $[e^{\lambda_1}-1]/[e^{\lambda_2}-1]$, not $\exp(\eta_1-\eta_2)$.
- So the shared observer-day effect cancels only in the rare-event limit.
- Grouse detection rates are a few percent, so the approximation is fine. But "cancels exactly" (D, E) is an overclaim.

### 4. Kill-rule logic and multiplicity

| Design | Rule | Problem |
|---|---|---|
| C X1, E T1 | Kill only if CC fails **and** the TkL CI includes 0 | A union pass: two chances to survive |
| D T2, E T1 | Pass at "> 1 SE" | One-sided α ≈ 0.16 per comparison |
| B | Holm adjustment on a conjunction of gates that must all pass | An intersection–union test already holds level α without adjustment. Holm only costs power, on fall-only data that is already thin (my estimate is about 1–2k complete-checklist fall detections in HH, so the TkL₅ SE is about 0.15). B errs toward false kills, which is the safer error, but its 0.55 prior for passing G1 is optimistic |

## Per-design notes

### A, CYM-H

**Likelihood.** The cloglog footprint is correct: $1-\exp(-e^{g}\sum K D)$ with a normalised kernel mean. The physics scale checks out: 2W·v = 30 m × 2 km/h = 6 ha/h.

**Identifiability.**
- Guild multipliers enter inside $\log(K*q)$, so a common scale is absorbed by $\beta_0$. They are identified only through the prior. That is minor.
- The alternation with `init_score` is fine.

**Test validity.**
- **T2's symmetric 2 × 2 rule is the best-designed injection test of the five.**
- But $n_0$ is **tuned on HH-X, and the ship rule is then decided on HH-X**. That is selection on the decision set, so the "beats the GBM twin on HH-X" gate is optimistically biased.
- HH-X (support π < 0.3) is small by construction. A concedes it may be untestable.

**Most serious unaddressed flaw.** The 5.5 km HH buffer removes about 98% of non-HH training checklists (finding 1). **BLOCKING as written**, though it is fixable by cross-fitting. T5's non-identifiability (finding 2) runs close behind.

### B, CEM-X

**Likelihood.** The LSE footprint objective is correct: the row gradient times the softmax share. Its per-row Hessian is a diagonal approximation (LOW). It has a finite-difference test and a stable fallback.

**Test validity: the strongest of the five.**
- Every decision is taken on fall checklists in HH, so a drumming map cannot pass by spring audibility. No identification assumption is needed for this.
- The cross-play margin $m^\star$ is calibrated on symmetric injected truths.
- The panel test is cross-fitted.
- The permutation band is a real computed null.
- It is explicit about what each detectability handle cannot identify.
- E's critique ("the shrunk fall head cannot fail") hits B less than the others, because B *decides on fall evaluation*, not on agreement between f and f + Δ.

**Weaknesses.**
- Holm on a conjunction (finding 4).
- The B5 panel has no leads or pre-trend placebo, unlike C's.
- The H-b mode heads are shrunk.

**Most serious unaddressed flaw.** The panel's age support and pre-2016 feature vintages (finding 2), together with training loss from the buffer (finding 1). **MAJOR.**

### C, FLUSH-C

**Strengths.**
- The best causal design: a stacked event study with never-treated or not-yet-treated controls, leads as a placebo, region × year effects and a cross-fitted model check.
- The assumption ledger is exemplary.

**Weaknesses.**
- The model-check regression "observed change on $\Delta\hat\eta_\ell$ with site FE" is under-specified. Site FE are collinear with a per-site difference.
- The X1 kill rule is a union pass.
- The fall-only guard (Spearman ≥ 0.7) compares against a shrunk Δ.
- AlphaEarth is well gated: 2024–25 evaluation, a record-proximity check and an infrastructure probe. But it still adds leakage surface for an expected gain of +0.1–0.3× TkL at P = 0.3.

**Most serious unaddressed flaw.** X2 identifies only ages 5–11 years, with post-cut pre-period features (finding 2). Because X2 is C's headline differentiator, this is **MAJOR**.

### D, COVERT-X

**Strengths.**
- Triangulation by "what each test differences out" is the right causal framing.
- The case-crossover is a genuine within-observer-day design.
- The honest reinterpretation of the permutation null as "observer geography + effort" is correct.

**Weaknesses.**
- 1-SE gates (finding 4).
- The mode heads are shrunk deviations fitted with F frozen. So ρ(F, F + D_vis) ≥ ρ(F, F + D_aur) compares two numbers that are both near 1, and the result is decided by noise. E's critique stands.
- Mode labels are missing not at random.

**Most serious unaddressed flaw.** The detectability triangulation check is effectively non-falsifiable: shrunk heads, and no injection calibration of the mode rule. **MAJOR.** Findings 1 and 2 also apply.

### E, FLUSH-E

**Strengths.**
- The independently fitted, unshrunk fall-only model $H_{\text{fall}}$ is the single best statistical answer to the cannot-fail problem.
- The triad threshold is calibrated by injection: it must fire on a tilted truth and stay silent on an untilted one in more than 90% of 50 draws. This applies PA-0021(c) properly, and noise-driven firing is controlled.

**Weaknesses.**
- **A false identification claim.** E says β is "identified only by spring-vs-fall contrasts at the same places". That is wrong. $\mathbb 1_{\text{spring}}\beta^\top o$ next to a season-free $H(\bar Z)$ that also contains the openness covariates is identified by the **cross-sectional** difference in openness slope between seasons, across all places.
  - So the day-1 power rule (fewer than 500 cross-season repeat locations → drop (a)) measures the wrong thing.
  - Seasonal habitat-use shifts confound β everywhere, not just at the same places.
- The T1 kill rule is a union pass (finding 4).

**Most serious unaddressed flaw.** The cross-sectional identification of β (above). **MEDIUM**, because the triad and $H_{\text{fall}}$ backstop it. Finding 1 is MAJOR for E as for every design.

## Vote: B > E > C > D > A

**1st, B.** From an identification standpoint, CEM-X is the only design where every decision is taken on a statistic that a wrong model can actually fail:
- fall-only HH evaluation, which needs no detectability identification assumption;
- a cross-play margin calibrated on symmetric injected truths;
- a computed permutation null;
- a cross-fitted panel.

It also states the exact limit of each detectability handle. Its errors lean conservative: Holm on a conjunction makes false kills more likely, not false ships. For a hunter, a wrongly shipped drumming or birder-geography map is the costlier error. It needs the cross-fitted HH fix (finding 1) and C's event-study leads.

**5th, A.** The trust-region idea is the only design that addresses off-support prediction, and its 2 × 2 injection rule is excellent. But as written:
- the 5.5 km buffer leaves about 2% of the training data;
- its signature within-site age curve (T5) cannot be identified from 2016–2025 checklists, which see only ages up to about 8 years;
- $n_0$ is tuned on the same HH-X subset that decides the ship rule.

It also has the heaviest build: a 35-parameter structure, an alternating fit and a Laplace approximation. Its expected precision gain over the GBM twin is "a tie" on the data that exist.

## Elements the winner (B) should adopt
1. **E's independent, unshrunk fall-only model $H_{\text{fall}}$**, with the injection-calibrated fire/no-fire rule, as the detectability decision. It replaces B's shrunk mode heads.
2. **C's stacked event study with leads (pre-trend placebo) and not-yet-treated controls** for B5. Predicted change is computed from the clock only and restricted to cut cohorts with true pre-cut features (finding 2).
