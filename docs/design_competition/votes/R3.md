## Facts checked in the code that bear on every design

1. **The HH arena geometry.**
   - `BLOCK_SIZE_M = 3000`.
   - `prepare_training_data.assign_spatial_blocks` lists only blocks occupied by thinned GBIF positives from 2020 on. It fills validation in hash order until it holds 20% of the records.
   - Every other block is assigned by `regions.block_split`'s md5 rule, with vf set to the validation share of the listed blocks.
   - `generate_negatives.py` uses `block_split`, so the CNN's validation set covers unoccupied blocks too. **HH must therefore be built with `block_split`.** If HH is built from `block_assignments.csv` alone, it contains only blocks that already hold a grouse report, which selects the arena on the outcome. No design says which it uses (shared, MEDIUM).

2. **Training-exclusion buffers around HH remove most of the training data. No design measured this.** I simulated a random 20% of 3 km blocks as validation:

   | Flat buffer | Share of non-HH land left for training | Designs |
   |---|---|---|
   | 2.5 km | ~30% | C, D, E |
   | 2.9 km | ~22% | — |
   | 5.5 km | **~2%** | A |

   Checklists cluster near hotspots, and validation blocks are dense where positives are dense. The loss is therefore heaviest exactly where the labels are, and what is left is skewed toward remote, rarely birded land. B's per-checklist, footprint-aware exclusion (truncated footprint plus 0.5 km) loses far less, because most checklists are short.

3. **Raster years.**
   - `YEAR_MIN = 2020` (`regions.py:73`).
   - `train.filter_by_year_gap` *refuses* (it does not drop) any record with no raster within ±2 years for some feature.
   - TreeMap vintages are 2016, 2020 and 2022; the 15 layers on disk cover 2016–2025.

   Every design whose panel test uses checklists from 2010–2015 and cuts from 2005–2019 (B5, C X2, D route 2, E X2) needs $\hat F(x_{\text{pre}})$ for years that have no legacy rasters and no AlphaEarth (which starts in 2017). In practice the "model-predicted within-site change" then comes **only from the clock features**. The panel validates the clock, not $F$. None of B, C, D or E states this (shared, MAJOR for C, whose centrepiece this is).

4. **`diagnose_gbm_baseline.py` produces *categorical* centre codes**: evt, evh, evc, sclass, fdist and nlcd, each re-coded to at most 254 levels. D and E "stencil-average" this design. The mean of EVT codes over 13–16 points is meaningless, so the categorical features must become one-hot shares first. B keeps one row per stencil point, so it avoids this. C reads the legacy block at a disc, which is ambiguous.

5. **Cloglog algebra.** I checked the derivatives of the row-wise loss.
   - The negative log-likelihood is convex in η. Its gradient is $e^\eta(1-y/p)$, which matches D's formula.
   - The Hessian is $\Lambda$ when $y=0$. When $y=1$ it is $\Lambda(\Lambda e^\Lambda-e^\Lambda+1)/(e^\Lambda-1)^2\approx\Lambda/2$ for small $\Lambda$. It is non-negative but tiny when detections are rare, so `min_sum_hessian_in_leaf` needs care.
   - **For a grouped log-sum-exp (LSE) objective, the per-row diagonal Hessian is $H s_m^2 + G s_m(1-s_m)$, where $s_m$ is the row's softmax share.** For a detection, $G\approx-1$ and $H\approx\Lambda/2$, so this is **negative on almost every detection row**. In LightGBM the leaf value is $-\sum G/(\sum H+\lambda)$, so it flips sign or explodes. A Gauss–Newton clamp, using $H s_m^2$ only, is required.
   - B, D (stage 2) and A (implicitly) depend on this. B's finite-difference test checks **gradients only**, so it will not catch it.

## Most serious unaddressed flaw per design

**A: BLOCKING as written; fixable.**
- **The flaw.** "Training excludes checklists within 5.5 km of an HH block." On the real 3 km / 20% split this leaves about 2% of non-HH land, skewed toward remote, unbirded forest. The GBM twin and CYM-H would both be fitted on a remnant, and the head-to-head on HH-X would be meaningless.
- **Runner-up (MAJOR).** $n_0$ is "chosen on the held-out extrapolation subset of HH", and HH-X is also the decisive ship test against the GBM twin. CYM-H gets a knob tuned on the test set that the twin does not get. $n_0$ must be tuned on the 25 km selection blocks.
- **Also unaddressed:**
  - A residual LightGBM inside $\log\sum_s K_i(s)D(s)$ needs the grouped LSE objective with the negative-Hessian problem above. A never specifies its gradient or Hessian, and calls it a simple `init_score` fit.
  - T5 cannot identify the descending limb of the age hump from EBD 2016–2025 alone, because post-cut ages are ≤ 9 years.
  - AlphaEarth is admitted only through a permutation gate, with no 2024–25 restriction. The permutation null cannot detect memorised report geography.

**B: MAJOR.**
- **The flaw.** The grouped cloglog-LSE objective as specified ("row-m gradient is the checklist gradient times its softmax share") yields negative per-row Hessians on detection rows. The committed unit test checks gradients only. A sign-flipped or unstable boost could pass the test and ship.
- **Mitigation already in B.** The kernel-mean fallback limits the damage.
- **Fix.** Use a Gauss–Newton Hessian $H s_m^2$, clamped at ≥ ε, and add a Hessian finite-difference test.
- **Secondary.** B5 uses checklists from 2010 on, but §3.1 lists EBD 2016–2025. The pre-2016 $\hat F$ problem is fact 3 above.

**C: MAJOR.**
- **The flaw.** One row per checklist reads every feature "at the radius nearest $\rho_\varsigma+L_j/2$". The same columns therefore mean 60 m on one row and 2,400 m on another, and $F$ is never told the scale. At prediction, the standard 2 km fall walk snaps to the **1,200 m** disc. Coverts of 2–40 ha (80–360 m across) inside the same ~1 km neighbourhood get near-identical scores. That caps hunter-scale precision regardless of AUC.
- **Also MAJOR.** X2's $\hat F(x_{\text{pre}})$ has no legacy rasters before 2016 and no AlphaEarth before 2017 (fact 3), and the 2.5 km buffer leaves only ~30% of training land.
- **Credit.** C's record-proximity check is the only AlphaEarth guard that addresses *spatial* memorisation. A 2024–25 time split alone does not, because grouse are reported at the same sites year after year.

**D: MAJOR.**
- **The flaw.** It has the same radius-mixed disc features and the same coarse prediction support ($\bar x^{\rho_{\text{fall}}}$, snapped to 1,200 m) as C. It also stencil-averages the categorical codes from `diagnose_gbm_baseline.py`.
- **Also open.**
  - 2.5 km buffer (~70% data loss).
  - The "> 1 bootstrap SE" kill rule, which B raised.
  - Stage 2 inherits B's Hessian issue.

**E: MAJOR.**
- **Change of support.** $H$ is trained on 16-point stencil means $\bar Z_j$, with σ ≈ 716 m for a 2 km walk. The estimand evaluates $h_{\text{fall}}(Z(s,t))$ at **single pixels**.
  - Pixel covariates have far higher variance than footprint means.
  - Prediction therefore pushes many cells off the training support, where the trees clamp to edge leaves. This is exactly where extreme young-dense-cover pixels, the hunter targets, live.
  - Fix: predict with the same stencil mean around $s$, or use the MIL stage.
- **Also open.** Stencil-averaged categorical codes, and the 2.5 km buffer.

## Vote

**1st: B (CEM-X).** B has the most coherent learning problem.
- **A pixel-level habitat function.** $F$ is learned on stencil points through a likelihood that averages $\exp F$ over the footprint, which is true multiple-instance learning. The fitted function lives at pixel scale, so prediction at covert resolution needs no change of support. C, D and E all lack this.
- **The best leakage controls of the five:**
  - HH by start point;
  - footprint-aware training exclusion, the only buffer that does not discard most of the data;
  - a panel test cross-fitted with treated sites excluded;
  - AlphaEarth confined to 2024–25;
  - a permutation null;
  - fall-gated decisions with Holm adjustment.
- **The defect is local and has a fallback.** The Hessian problem is a two-line repair, and B already pre-commits to E's kernel-mean variant if the grouped objective proves fragile.

**5th: A (CYM-H).** A is the only design that confronts off-support extrapolation at prediction time, and I would like that idea to survive. As engineered, though, it is the least correct.
- **The training buffer.** The 5.5 km HH buffer leaves about 2% of the data.
- **Test-set tuning.** The trust-region scale $n_0$ is tuned on the same HH-X subset that decides the ship rule.
- **An unspecified objective.** The residual-inside-a-log-sum objective is presented as an `init_score` fit, with no gradient or Hessian given.
- **The age curve.** It is "estimated within sites" from a data window too short to see the descending limb.
- **AlphaEarth.** It enters with a gate that cannot detect report memorisation.
- **Feasibility.** It also has the heaviest stack for one person (cube, lidar, L-BFGS/GBM/GAM alternation, support model) during an active season.

## Elements the winner should adopt

1. **E's independent, unshrunk fall-only model $H_{\text{fall}}$** as the reference for spring/fall agreement. B's $\Delta_{\text{fall}}$ is depth ≤ 3 with strong L2, so its agreement check cannot fail.
2. **A's HH-X extrapolation subset**, with any support-shrinkage tuned on the 25 km selection blocks, not on HH. It should be reported alongside **C's record-proximity AlphaEarth check**, so that off-support behaviour and spatial memorisation are both measured rather than only flagged.
