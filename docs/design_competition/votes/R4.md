## 2. New quantitative finding: training-exclusion buffers discard most of the data

HH is the set of checklists that start in validation blocks. Validation blocks are about 20% of 3 km blocks. Occupied blocks are taken in hash order up to `round(0.2 N)`, and positive-free blocks become validation by an md5 hash at the same fraction (`prepare_training_data.py:344-367`, `regions.py:270-286`). That makes them effectively random.

I ran a Monte Carlo of a uniform point in a training block against random 20% validation blocks (script in scratchpad `buf.py`). It gives the share of non-HH checklists that **survive** the training exclusion:

| Buffer around HH blocks | Retained |
|---|---|
| 1.0 km | ~70% |
| 2.0 km | ~39% |
| **2.5 km (C, D, E)** | **~31%** |
| 2.9 km (B's worst case: footprint ≤ 2.4 km + 0.5 km) | ~22% |
| **5.5 km (A)** | **~2%** |

- **A as written trains on about 2% of checklists.** Its GBM residual, its support model and the T6 ship rule are then starved.
- **C, D and E throw away about 69%.** That cuts the already scarce fall detections (about 15% of records) to roughly a third.
- **B's footprint-aware rule** drops long traveling checklists preferentially. That re-creates in training the representativeness bias B fixed in HH.

Every critic in rounds 2–3 pushed buffers *larger*, and none computed the cost. Possible fixes:
- exclude only footprint *cells* that overlap HH, by masking stencil points rather than whole checklists;
- or redefine HH as validation blocks with an internal 1 km inset.

## 5. Most serious unaddressed flaw per design

- **A: BLOCKING as written (a one-parameter fix).** The 5.5 km training exclusion around HH keeps about 2% of non-HH checklists. Separately, T5 cannot identify the 10-yr age peak from 2016–2025 data, so the within-site age curve, A's key differentiator, falls back to between-site. A also carries the largest build of the five (lidar at terabyte scale, an 11-year cube, 10 modules) against a 19 Oct switch.
- **B: MAJOR.** Every gate must pass on fall-only HH checklists, Holm-adjusted.
  - Fall is about 15% of detections, and HH is about 20% of checklists, so fall same-day discordant pairs in HH will likely number in the tens to low hundreds. CC's SE is then about 0.05, against an expected gain of 0.03–0.05.
  - The battery is built to fail for lack of power, not because the model is wrong. That kills a working programme.
  - B's T-1 demotion threshold (< 2,000 pairs) is on all-season pairs, not fall.
- **C: MAJOR.** X2, C's centrepiece, cannot be run as described:
  - cuts in 2005–2009 have no pre-window;
  - the pre-period has no legacy or TCC rasters, so the out-of-sample ΔF̂ collapses to the clock alone.

  Second: the embedding block means about 700 GB of 10 m downloads, the EE catalog ends in 2024, and the "2024–25" gate is effectively 2024 only.
- **D: MEDIUM.** The decisive T2 is scheduled for days 2–4, but D's own P0–P3 needs about 11 working days first. Also, the 2.5 km buffer discards about 69% of training checklists, and same-day pairs need both start points inside scattered HH blocks, which makes pairs scarce. The T0 count and fallback partly cover this last point.
- **E: MAJOR.** The forecast back-test ("freeze features at 2020, age the clock to 2024") cannot freeze at 2020. The LANDFIRE legacy layers start in 2022 and already show 2021–22 cuts, so the back-test leaks future state and is biased toward passing. This is exactly the 2016–2025 class of error. E also has the 2.5 km buffer loss, which is worse for E because its unshrunk H_fall trains on fall data only.
- **Shared by all five (MAJOR).**
  - 2016–2019 checklists have no legacy rasters within tolerance.
  - OPERA DIST-ALERT is not in Earth Engine.
  - Nobody budgets the CR-0035 execution as the critical path before the legacy block exists.

## 6. Vote

**BALLOT: D,E,B,C,A**

**1st, D (COVERT-X).**
- It is the only design whose implementation plan states effort per CR-scoped phase (P0–P4, about 15–16 days). That plan fits one owner who must finish CR-0035 first.
- Its stage-1 model is a plain one-row LightGBM-cloglog plus a GAM on CPU. Every expensive or uncertain data source (AlphaEarth, MIL, ARU, Current Use GIS) is optional and marked unverified where it is.
- Its gates are counted on day 1 with stated fallbacks.
- Its flaws are fixable without redesign: the inconsistent "days 2–4" T2 date, and the buffer cost.

**5th, A (CYM-H).**
- A's off-support idea is the best new idea in the field. But it is the least deliverable design.
- As written it trains on about 2% of checklists.
- Its "site-selection-free" age curve is unidentifiable from 2016–2025 within-site ages.
- It schedules a lidar build at terabyte scale, an 11-year habitat cube and ten new modules against a 19 Oct switch.
- Most of its expected gain rests on HH-X, whose size is unknown until week 1.

## 7. Elements the winner should adopt

1. **A's support-weighted residual shrinkage and "data-driven vs ecology-driven" badge**, as a later optional CR on top of D's stage 1. This is the only prediction-time answer to off-trail extrapolation.
2. **C's 2024–25 time-split arm (train ≤ 2023).** It is a free, leak-resistant check on every claim, and it sidesteps the pre-2020 raster gap.

Also: replace the flat 2.5 km checklist exclusion with stencil-cell masking, and build the v0 H250 layer from the `tsd` rasters already on disk.
