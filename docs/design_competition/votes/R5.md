## 2. Shared assumptions attacked (the convergence blind spots)

**S1. eBird encounter rank equals hunter flush rank.** No design can falsify this in season 1.
- The only hunter truths are:
  - S0: n ≈ 30, SE ≈ 0.18, and biased toward visible young cover;
  - the randomised fifth covert: about 40 visits, which detects only a 2× lift.
- Every ship decision is therefore made on eBird metrics alone.
- The transfer term $W(s)$ is fitted from season 2 in every design.
- The designs differ only in what they ship when the assumption is wrong. A's off-support predictions lean on ecology, not on birder data, so A is the least exposed. B, C, D and E only badge the problem.

**S2. Fall eBird volume.**
- From the counts above, HH holds about 20% of blocks, which leaves roughly 800 Oct–Nov detections over ten years. Before the complete-checklist filter, Sep–Dec gives about 1,000–1,400.
- The top 5% of forest then sees on the order of 10² detections.
- Fall TkL₅ therefore has a block-bootstrap SE of roughly 0.2–0.3×, which is about the size of the claimed CNN gap (1.3–1.8× vs 1.6–2.4×).
- Consequences by design:
  - B gates *every* decision on fall, with Holm adjustment, so B is the most likely to kill a working programme for lack of power.
  - E's independent H_fall is the most data-starved model.
  - D and C use fall TkL as primary or co-primary.
- "Sep–Dec" also mixes September (pre-shuffle) and December (winter conifer roosting). Neither matches October cover. No design noticed this.

**S3. TkL₅ can be gamed, and it rewards the wrong space.**
- Observed ÷ effort-expected detections in the top 5% is defined only where checklists fall.
- A map that puts its top 5% in unbirded, off-trail regeneration gets few checklists there, a noisy lift, and fails the gate. That off-trail regeneration is exactly what the hunter wants.
- A map that puts its top 5% on birded young growth near trails gets a tight, high lift.
- The denominator ($g$) contains no location term, so known-grouse trails inside HH inflate the lift whenever their habitat proxies generalise.
- So the shared primary metric selects *for* in-support ranking.
- The case-crossover CC has the same limit: it compares two **birded** sites.
- Mitigations:
  - A's HH-X (support < 0.3) is the only arena aimed at the hunter's space;
  - C and E report off-trail importance-weighted TkL, but clip the weights at [0.2, 5], which caps the correction exactly where it matters;
  - B and D do nothing here.

**S4. The year-gap lesson recurs, and all five missed it.**
- All five fit EBD 2016–2025 using the "existing 15 layers + `mch_*`".
  - For 2016–2019 checklists, LANDFIRE does not exist. The nearest vintage is 2022, a gap of 3–6 years, beyond `YEAR_MATCH_TOLERANCE`.
  - So a 2017 checklist is described by a post-2019 stand. For any stand cut between visit and vintage, that is **future information**.
  - `mch_*` (2018–20 imagery) likewise shows cuts from 2021–25 as tall canopy.
  - A's claim that "Checklist features are read at stencil points for the checklist's own year" is true only for the clock layers.
- **Panel tests are hit hardest.** B5, C X2, D route 2 and E X2 compute $\Delta\hat F=\hat F(x_{\text{post}})-\hat F(x_{\text{pre}})$ with pre-windows from 2010 onward.
  - The legacy block for $x_{\text{pre}}$ either does not exist or is the **post-cut** raster, so its contribution to $\Delta\hat F$ is identically zero.
  - The test then validates only the clock component, not "the model". It also silently feeds post-cut legacy covariates into pre-cut rows.
- **A's within-site age curve (T5) is undercut differently.** A's data are EBD 2016–2025 only (§3.4 O1; cube 2016–2026).
  - A location needs at least 8 checklists both before and after a cut, so cuts are effectively 2017–2023 and post-cut ages are at most about 8 years.
  - The age hump that A imports "as fixed" (prior $t_p\approx10$) is therefore extrapolated past the support of its own within-site estimator.
- Severity: **MAJOR (shared)**. It is not BLOCKING, because restricting fits to 2020+ or to clock-only $\Delta\hat F$ fixes it. But every design states the opposite.

**S5. Partial harvest.**
- Maine harvesting is predominantly partial (shelterwood/selection), and LCMS/Hansen stand-replacing loss largely misses it. All five list this as a shared MAJOR.
- Only A changes the *model* for it (an additive 1–6 m lidar understory term, masked after the flight date). The others rely on `mch_f15`, a static 2018–20 layer.

**S6. Hunting pressure and depletion.**
- Top-ranked A1 public coverts are where other hunters also go.
- Only A has a pressure term, and only in the hunt-log model. Every ranking is pre-pressure. Severity: MEDIUM, shared.

## 4. Single most serious unaddressed flaw per design

| Design | Flaw | Severity |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | **T5 cannot identify the age hump it imports as fixed.** The panel is limited to EBD 2016–2025 (§3.4 O1), so post-cut ages within site reach about 8 years. A peak near 10 years, and the whole descending limb, are prior-driven, yet A labels the result "within-site, free of site selection" and uses it as the extrapolation anchor where $\pi\to0$. That is the very region A claims to win. Fix: extend EBD to 2005+ for T5 only (the clock is buildable from 1985), or label $t_p$ as prior-dominated. The support model's calibration ($n_0$ tuned on HH-X, which is small by A's own T4) is a close second. | MAJOR |
| **B** | **The fall-gated, Holm-adjusted battery is likely underpowered on real fall volume** (S2). G1 "fails → stop the checklist programme" can kill a working programme for lack of power. T0 states the power but does not change the kill rule into "no decision". B also has no remedy for off-support prediction, only a badge. | MAJOR |
| **C** | **The X2 model check is mostly a clock check** (S4). Pre-period legacy and embedding features do not exist before 2016–2017 (AlphaEarth starts in 2017), so the "out-of-sample model predicts within-site change" claim overstates what is tested. E's forest→forest land-use filter and D's route-change confound remain unadopted. | MAJOR |
| **D** | **The detection-mode route can switch the ranking to $F+D_{\text{vis}}$, whose labels are summer brood and road codes** (FL/DD/NY/CF from June–July; "on road" comments). The mode heads are also shrunk, so $\rho_{\text{mode}}$ is close to guaranteed to agree (E's point). One route cannot fail, and the other, if it fires, promotes road-edge coverts. Add the 1-SE one-sided kill rule (about 16% false pass per test) and D is the design most likely to ship a confidently wrong map. | MAJOR |
| **E** | **The season-contrast term assumes the same λ in spring and fall** (raised by A, B and C; not answered). T2 plants no use-shift truth, so β absorbs real fall-shuffle habitat change as "spring detectability" along the openness axis, which is the young-cover axis. It is mitigated, not fixed, by the independent H_fall stack. H_fall itself rests on about 10² HH fall detections (S2). | MEDIUM |

## 5. Vote

**1st: A (CYM-H).**
- Under the skeptic's assumption that every design is wrong, the question is what each ships when it is wrong in the place that matters. Every design is fitted where birders walk, and the hunter's target is where they do not.
- Four designs answer that with a badge. A alone changes the prediction: support-weighted shrinkage toward an ecological structure.
- It pre-registers a symmetric, falsifiable test of that choice: the T2 2×2 cross scored on unvisited cells.
- It also pre-registers a collapse to the plain GBM twin if the bet fails. Its downside is therefore bounded by the shared-core model everyone else ships.
- A is also the only design that:
  - models partial-harvest understory directly;
  - builds a hunter-pressure term;
  - evaluates on an extrapolation arena (HH-X) rather than only in-support TkL.
- Its costs are real: the most code, a support model of uncertain calibration, and a T5 claim it cannot support with 2016+ data (§4). None of these makes it worse than the GBM twin it can fall back to.

**5th: D (COVERT-X).**
- D is a solid shared core with the weakest protection against shared-assumption failure.
- Its unique route, detection mode, is built on summer brood and road-gravel labels with shrunk heads. As a check it cannot fail, and when it does fire it can re-rank toward road edges.
- Its 1-SE kill rule is the loosest gate in the field.
- It has no off-support remedy and no off-trail weighting.
- Like B, C and E, it inherits S4 in its panel route.
- B is safer, because it fails closed to H250 when underpowered. D can fail open.

## 6. Elements the winner should adopt

1. **E's independent, unshrunk H_fall with the injection-calibrated agreement rule.** It is the one detectability check across the five designs that can actually fail. Use it as A's fall-transfer guard in place of the shrunk $\Delta_{\text{fall}}$.
2. **C's stacked event-study panel**, with leads as a pre-trend placebo and the model refitted without panel sites, **extended to EBD 2005+ and run on clock features only.** This would make A's T5 genuinely identify the descending limb of the age hump, and remove the S4 contamination.
