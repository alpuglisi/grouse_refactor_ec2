# R7 review: decision analyst / product owner lens

Lens criterion (e): **expected value and speed of learning.** The formula is P(gates pass) × hunter value if they pass, plus the residual value kept on failure, minus the build cost and the time to the first useful map in the 2026 season. Today is 2026-10-05, and the season runs to about 31 Dec, so roughly 12 weeks remain.

All five proposals were read in full. Facts below were checked against the repository; no repository file was edited.

## Newly found flaw, shared and missed by all three critique rounds (MAJOR for B–E, MEDIUM for A)

**The legacy feature block is read "for the checklist's own year", but it does not exist for most of the 2016–2025 fitting window.**

- B, C, D and E fit F on 2016–2025 checklists with the `diagnose_gbm_baseline.py` legacy design. A uses it in its residual.
- For 2016–2021 checklists, about 60% of the window, the LANDFIRE structure layers silently resolve to 2022. That is up to 6 years off, three times the repo's own tolerance. `mch_*` is one epoch for every year.
- **Failure scenario:** a stand cut in 2019 reads as short or young canopy (`ch`, `evh`, `mch_f15`) for its 2016–2018 pre-cut checklists. This attenuates and biases exactly the age/height response hunters need.
- **Consequence for the panel tests** (B5, C X2, D route 2, E X2, which use pre-windows back to 2010): F̂(x_pre) has no legacy features at all. The "model-predicted within-site change" is then purely the clock component. The test cannot validate the full F it claims to validate.
- A grep of round3/*.md shows no design mentions the 2022 LANDFIRE floor.
- **Fix:** either use per-year validity channels with legacy features only where |gap| ≤ 2 (and clock + TreeMap-nearest otherwise), or fit legacy features on 2020+ only. Then restate the panel check as a clock-component check.

## Each design's most serious unaddressed flaw

- **A (MAJOR): the distinctive bet is probably untestable this season, and the ship rule then defaults to the GBM twin.**
  - HH-X is defined as low-support dense young off-trail cover. By construction it is where checklists are rare. A itself admits it may hold fewer than 200 detections, in which case "the field becomes decisive", yet the field detects only ≥ 2×.
  - `n0` is also tuned on that tiny subset.
  - The owner pays for the largest build of the five: 10 modules, a 50-band cube, Laplace alternation, and lidar PDAL for 3 states. The 2026 product is then most likely the GBM twin.
  - A's T5 panel is also confined to the 2016+ cube, while B–E use 2010+, so it is likely underpowered. A also carries the legacy-vintage flaw in its residual.
- **B (MAJOR): the false-kill probability is high.** Every gate must pass on **fall-only HH checklists** (≈15% of detections × 20% of blocks), Holm-adjusted across six gates. The likely outcome is that the checklist programme stops, or ships late at week 3, when the effect is real but underpowered. That is low EV for a strong design. Add the legacy-vintage flaw.
- **C (MAJOR): the legacy-vintage flaw makes X2 a test of the clock only.** X2, C's headline within-site test, uses pre-windows to 2010. F̂(x_pre) has no LANDFIRE, TCC or TreeMap inputs before 2016, and no LANDFIRE before 2022. A second issue (MEDIUM): its DT guard compares against a "fall-only" model whose independence from the shrunk Δ_fall is not stated (E's cannot-fail critique).
- **D (MAJOR):**
  - The mode heads remain "shallow, shrunk" deviations (§3.6). Both ρ(F, F+D_vis) and ρ(F, F+D_aur) are therefore ≈1, and the pre-registered triangulation inequality compares noise. E raised this and D did not answer it.
  - The schedule is also internally inconsistent. T2 is "decisive on days 2–4", but P0–P3 are ≈ 11–12 days of serial CR work. The day-1 H250 needs the P2 disturbance fusion (3–4 d).
- **E (MAJOR): the legacy-vintage flaw.** "Stencil features, own year per checklist" cannot hold for 2016–2021 LANDFIRE/`mch`. This matters most for E's independent H_fall, which has the least data and is the most sensitive to stale structure features. Smaller issue (MEDIUM): T1 is scheduled "days 3–5", but P0–P3 sum to ≈ 12 days.

## Vote and justification

**1st: E (FLUSH-E).** It has the highest expected value per unit of owner effort.
- It has the lightest stack, an H250 map this week, and a day-1 experiment (scoring the CNN vs H250 vs effort-only on HH) that tells the owner whether today's map has within-landscape skill before anything is fitted.
- Its kill rule requires both CC and fall TkL to fail. That lowers the false-kill risk that destroys EV in B.
- Its one unique piece, the independently fitted fall model inside an injection-calibrated triad with an "always stack" fallback, attacks the most hunter-relevant failure (a drumming map). It is also the only detectability check designed so that it can fail.
- Its forecast back-test and rule-out layer add product value at almost no cost.

**5th: A (CYM-H).** Its hunter thesis is the most attractive: ecology carries the map where birders never walk. But the expected value for 2026 is the lowest.
- It has the largest build and the highest slip risk against its own 19 Oct switch date.
- The test of its distinctive bet (HH-X) will probably be too small to decide anything.
- Its pre-registered ship rule therefore most likely ships the GBM twin, which B–E deliver at a fraction of the cost.
- The owner learns least about what is unique to A this season while paying the most.

1. **From A:** the **support badge and HH-X coverage audit** as a reported (not gating) diagnostic, plus a pre-registered **switch date** from H250 to the fitted map. This turns "week 3" into a commitment.
2. **From C:** the **stacked event-study X2 with leads as a pre-trend placebo**, out-of-sample refit with a buffer. E already adds a forest-to-forest filter. Restate it as a clock-component check, given the legacy-vintage flaw above.
